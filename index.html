Through the Lens: Tokyo

Where do visitors point their cameras in Tokyo, and what cameras are they holding?

Planning a trip to Tokyo? Every geotagged photo records where its owner stood. Together, they show which parts of the city draw the crowds and how people move between them. We then look at the cameras themselves: what they cost, how they changed and how brands compare.

Quick terms

Geotag
A photo’s saved location.
Megapixels
Millions of dots in a photo; more means finer detail.
Zoom (focal length)
How close a lens brings a faraway scene.
Log scale
Each step multiplies the value, so cheap and expensive items fit on one axis.

Act II · The camera

What Does a Camera Cost?

Behind every photo is a camera, from budget compacts to professional bodies. Both charts use a log scale for price.

Camera price by release year

Each circle is one camera; bigger circles have more megapixels.

Price range within each brand

Each box covers the middle half of a brand’s prices; the dark line is the median (middle) price. Dots are unusual prices.

How Cameras Changed Over Time

Over the years, prices shifted, new models multiplied and sensors grew sharper.

Median price evolution

The median is the middle price, so extreme models don’t distort it.

Model releases per year

New releases climbed from a handful in the mid-1990s to 163 in 2007.

Resolution evolution trend

Resolution is the number of pixels in a photo. Higher resolution lets you crop or print a Shibuya night shot without blur.

What Is in the Camera Bag?

Zoom decides whether you can capture Tokyo Tower from across the park; storage decides which cards to pack.

Lens zoom vs price density

Darker cells hold more cameras.

Memory included with the camera

Each square is 1% of models with a memory card. Almost a third include 16 MB.

Cheapest to priciest model by brand

Each line runs from a brand’s cheapest model (grey) to its priciest (black); the orange diamond is the average. Premium brands sit at the top, budget brands at the bottom.

Putting It Together

Tokyo is not photographed evenly: visitors gather in a few hotspots and move between neighbours. The cameras behind those photos range from simple to professional, and grew sharper and more varied over time.
