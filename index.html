Through the Lens: Tokyo

An interactive data story exploring tourist mobility across Tokyo and photography equipment trends.

Overview: Designed for urban tourism analysts and photography enthusiasts, this dashboard combines spatial geotagged photo footprints across Tokyo with technical camera specifications. The goal is to uncover where visitors congregate, how they navigate between landmarks, and how camera hardware supports urban photography.

Act I: Spatial & Geographic Dynamics

Every day, thousands of visitors explore Tokyo, leaving behind digital footprints via geotagged photographs. By classifying photo longitudes into municipal zones, we observe that over 65% of all geotagged photos concentrate within the Central Core Wards (Shinjuku, Shibuya, Minato), while Waterfront and Western zones serve as secondary excursion routes. Filtering by tourist ID reveals individual daily routes—some visitors stay within tight localized hubs, while others execute cross-city journeys.

Spatial Magnitude and Density

Scaling photo markers by spatial coordinate magnitude illustrates structural spatial weight. Rather than spreading evenly, tourist activities cling tightly to major transport arteries framing central Tokyo. Aggregating these into a 2D geographic grid eliminates point overlap, revealing two primary super-clusters: Shinjuku-Shibuya in the west and Ginza-Asakusa in the east.

Act II: Equipment Performance & Technology

Behind every photograph is an optical instrument. Dispersing raw camera bodies across ISO sensitivity bins reveals that entry-level gear tops out at ISO 6,400, whereas professional bodies push beyond ISO 25,600. Furthermore, plotting individual camera body prices on a logarithmic scale highlights the spread from budget point-and-shoots to high-end flagship systems.
